Neeps Group

Not a brochure site. A public web presence bolted onto a custom CRM and internal operations suite — leads, deals, inbox, HR and document generation — running as three coordinated Next.js applications over one Postgres database.

The brief

The visible half of this project is a marketing site: services, case studies, careers, team, blog. The half that took the work is what sits behind the login.

Enquiries were arriving through a contact form and then living in someone's inbox. There was no pipeline, no record of what had been quoted, and no link between a lead and the eventual client. Internal admin — leave requests, employee documents, team records — ran on paper and email.

So the build became one system: capture the lead on the public site, work it through a CRM, and run the business's internal paperwork in the same place.

What I built

Public site

Services, About, Team, Careers, Blog, Case Studies and a specialist interiors section, with the contact form writing directly into the CRM rather than sending an email into the void.

CRM and operations console

Leads & contacts

Every enquiry captured as a structured record, deduplicated against existing contacts, with source and status tracked.

Deals & customers

Leads promoted through a pipeline into deals and, once won, into customer records — so history survives the handover.

Inbox & reply

Threaded correspondence handled inside the console, with transactional sending so replies come from the business, not a personal account.

Team & leave

Employee records plus a leave application and approval flow, replacing the emailed form.

Document generation

Internal documents and forms rendered server-side to PDF, so a leave form or client document is generated rather than hand-filled.

Vouchers & analytics

Voucher issuing with QR encoding, and an analytics view over pipeline and enquiry volume.

Architecture

Three separate Next.js applications — the main group site, an IT division site, and an accounting division site — each deployable on its own, sharing one Postgres database through a common Prisma schema.

Design decision

Three apps rather than one, because the divisions brand and release independently — but one database, because a lead that arrives through the IT site is still a lead for the group. A single dev command boots all three together so local work never drifts out of sync.

The harder parts

Auth with real roles

Staff, admin and division-level access over the same console, using NextAuth with a Prisma adapter and middleware guarding the admin routes at the edge rather than per-page.

Documents that have to be right

Leave forms and internal documents are generated server-side as PDFs. Rendering to PDF on the server keeps output identical regardless of who opens it — no browser print-dialog variance on a document that goes into an employee file.

Mail that doesn't land in spam

Transactional sending through a dedicated provider with proper domain authentication, because a CRM whose replies get filtered is worse than no CRM.
